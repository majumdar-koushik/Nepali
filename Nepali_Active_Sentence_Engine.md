# Nepali Active Sentence Engine — RR

**RR = Nepali Ready Reckoner**

## Purpose

RR is a compact sentence-building reference for active Nepali production.

**Core idea:** Hindi sentence structure + Nepali postpositions/case markers + Nepali verb patterns + familiar shared vocabulary.

The aim is to generate new sentences from building blocks rather than memorise hundreds of complete sentences.

# 2. Important grammar distinctions

## Gender, number and respect — when forms change

Nepali does not mark gender in every verb form. Where a form changes with gender, number or respect, RR shows the variation. Do not add a gender distinction where the Nepali form is the same.

Common examples:

- **u gayo** → he went
- **u gai** → she went
- **uniharu gaye** → they went
- **uhã jaanuhunubhayo** → he/she went, respectful
- **gako / gaki / gaka** → forms that can vary according to the noun/person being described
- **bhayeko / bhayeki / bhayeka** → participial forms that can agree with the noun they describe

For forms that do not change for gender, the same form is used for male and female speakers/listeners.

## 4.1 ho vs chha

**ho** is primarily used for identity / classification.

**7. U shikshak ho.** → He/she is a teacher.

**chha** is used for location, state, condition and existence.

**8. U gharma chha.** → He/she is at home.

**9. Yo ramro chha.** → This is good.

This is a useful basic distinction, not an absolute rule for every Nepali construction.

## 4.2 bhayeko — usage spectrum

**bhayeko** is the participial form of **hunu**. It is not limited to the English idea of "has been". It can describe a noun, form relative-clause constructions, and occur in perfect/completed constructions.

**10. Nilo aankha bhayeko maanis.** → A person with blue eyes.

**11. Khairo kapal bhayeki tyo mahila.** → That woman with brown hair.

**12. Kampanima bhayeka sabai.** → Everyone who was / was present in the company.

**13. Uhaãle sujhaab garnu bhayeko bichaar.** → The idea that he/she suggested.

**14. Maile gareko kaam ramro chha.** → The work that I did is good.

**15. Ke bhayeko chha?** → What has happened?

**16. U doctor bhayeko chha.** → He/she has become a doctor.

**17. U doctor bhayeko thiyo.** → He/she had become a doctor.

**18. Mero aama buwa sanga basna bhayeko chaalis barsa bhai sakyo.** → It has been forty years since I started living with my parents.

**19. U bidhuwa bhayeko paanch barsa bhanda badi bhayeko chha.** → She has been a widow for more than five years.

**20. Tapai ahilesamma kahilyai tapuma jaanubhayeko chha?** → Have you ever been to the island?

**21. Uhaã chhittai nai badhuwaa hunubhayō.** → He/she was promoted very quickly.

**22. Janubhayeko** → respectful participial form related to **janu**.

**23. Garnubhayeko** → respectful participial form related to **garnu**.

**24. Basnubhayeko** → respectful participial form related to **basnu**.

The key question when seeing **bhayeko** is: **What noun, event or state is it describing, and what larger construction is it part of?**

# 11. Seven core tense patterns

## How to read this section

The six subjects are:

- **1st singular:** ma = I
- **1st plural:** hami = we
- **2nd singular:** timi = you
- **2nd plural:** timiharu = you all
- **3rd singular:** u = he/she
- **3rd plural:** uniharu = they

The examples use:

- **BE = hunu** ("to be / become")
- **DO = garnu** ("to do")

For **hunu**, continuous forms naturally express an ongoing process such as "becoming"; Nepali does not mechanically mirror English "being" in every tense. The ordinary state/location forms are covered by the **ho/chha** distinction in the grammar section.

### Gender and respect in the tense tables

The six core subjects below are deliberately neutral where Nepali is neutral. Gender variation is shown where the finite or participial form actually changes. Respectful forms are not simply ordinary forms with a polite pronoun; they can use different verb forms.

Examples of applicable variation:

- **U doctor thiyo.** → He was a doctor.
- **U doctor thii.** → She was a doctor.
- **U gayo.** → He went.
- **U gai.** → She went.
- **Uniharu gaye.** → They went.
- **Uhã jaanuhunubhayo.** → He/she went, respectfully.
- **U tayaar bhayeko chha.** → He has become ready.
- **U tayaar bhayeki chha.** → She has become ready.
- **Uhã gharma hunuhunchha.** → He/she is at home, respectfully.

Where the Nepali form does not change for gender, the same form is used for male and female. Respectful forms should be learned separately where Nepali uses a distinct form such as **-nuhunchha** or **-nubhayō**.

# 12. Core reusable patterns

This section contains **sentence-building patterns that are not simply ordinary tense conjugations**.

**172. verb + nuparne** → have to / must

- **Garnuparne** → have to do
- **Januparne** → have to go
- **Malai januparne chha.** → I have to go.

**173. verb + na saknu** → cannot / be unable to

- **Bolna sakdina.** → I cannot speak.
- **U bolna sakdaina.** → He/she cannot speak.

**174. malai + verb + nu + chha** → I need / have to

- **Malai janu chha.** → I have to go.
- **Malai kaam garnu chha.** → I have to work.

**175. malai + verb + na + dinuhos** → let me / allow me to

- **Malai garna dinuhos.** → Let me do it.

**176. verb + nu aghi** → before doing

- **Janu aghi malai bhannuhos.** → Tell me before you go.

**177. verb + nu bhanda aghi** → before doing
