# Nepali Connected-Language Study Progress

**Programme:** Paragraph comprehension, translation, listening and later speaking  
**Planned horizon:** Approximately six months; progress is performance-based, not calendar-based.  
**Primary long-term goal:** Read free Nepali online storybooks, beginning with children's-level material and progressing gradually toward more mature/adult-level stories, using Roman-script transcripts, Nepali audio, or both.

## Programme principles

- Roman Nepali exclusively in learning material and learner-facing transcripts. Never use Devanagari unless the learner explicitly requests it.
- Use natural, everyday Nepali and connected passages rather than isolated vocabulary drills.
- Do not reveal translations, transcripts or answers before the learner attempts the exercise.
- Encourage inference from context; explain only vocabulary or grammar that materially affects comprehension or production.
- Correct precisely. Distinguish errors from acceptable alternatives and naturalness improvements.
- Assess demonstrated ability, not exercise count. Recycle weak areas and vary contexts before advancing.
- Keep this programme separate from the Nepali Ready Reckoner (RR). Do not modify RR references, RR progress, or the existing sentence engine.
- Use project grammar references when a grammar point requires verification. Mark unsupported explanations as unverified rather than extrapolating.

## Learning components and permanent exercise IDs

| Component | ID prefix | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Written paragraph comprehension | R | Read Roman Nepali; learner explains meaning in English |
| English-to-Nepali paragraph translation | T | Learner translates connected English passages into Roman Nepali |
| Listening paragraph comprehension | L | Hear Nepali audio without seeing transcript; learner explains meaning in English |
| Speaking practice | S | Parked until deliberately introduced |

Each exercise receives a unique sequential ID (R-001, T-001, L-001, etc.). Keep separate progression decisions for each component.

## Progression framework

| Stage | Passage length | Expected challenge |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 3–4 sentences | Familiar vocabulary and grammar |
| 2 | 5–6 sentences | Varied tenses and constructions |
| 3 | 7–9 sentences | Less familiar vocabulary and connected ideas |
| 4 | 10–12 sentences | Natural everyday Nepali |

Apply stages independently to R, T and L. Passage length is a guide, not an automatic promotion rule. Later storybook work should move from simple children's stories to progressively richer narratives, based on demonstrated comprehension.

## Assessment and status definitions

- **Attempted:** Learner submitted a response; assessment may still be pending.
- **Learning:** Ability is developing; important gaps remain.
- **Competent:** Demonstrated reliably across more than one varied exercise at the current stage.
- **Mastered:** Demonstrated consistently across varied material and contexts.
- **Review:** Previously successful material needs recycling due to a newly observed weakness.
- **Not started:** No assessed attempt yet.

For each attempt, record date, exercise ID, stage, passage length, learner response, assessment, errors/missed information, useful vocabulary or structures (only when needed), status and next decision. Do not use a single score as the sole basis for promotion.

## Exercise formats

### R — Written comprehension
1. Present a short Roman Nepali paragraph only.
2. Learner explains the overall meaning in English.
3. Assess meaning, sequence, key details and inferred meaning.
4. Do not routinely ask redundant comprehension questions after a complete paragraph explanation. Ask targeted follow-up questions only if clarification or a specific diagnostic check is useful.
5. Give corrections and concise explanations after the attempt.

### T — Paragraph translation
1. Present a short English paragraph.
2. Learner translates it into Roman Nepali.
3. Review sentence by sentence.
4. Separate grammatical errors, wrong word choices, unnatural phrasing and acceptable alternatives.
5. Explain only the corrections needed.

### L — Listening comprehension
1. Provide actual spoken Nepali audio, with no visible transcript before the attempt.
2. Learner explains what was understood in English.
3. Assess comprehension; ask targeted questions only when useful.
4. Only after the attempt, provide the Roman Nepali transcript and explain relevant missed words/expressions.
5. Never silently substitute a reading exercise for listening. If actual audio cannot be delivered in the current interface, state the limitation and agree a practical audio/voice-mode alternative before counting an L exercise.

### S — Speaking (parked)
Do not start until the learner explicitly agrees. Later scope: spontaneous answers, everyday descriptions, past/future narration, opinions and natural conversation.

## Long-term storybook pathway

The destination is independent reading/listening to free Nepali stories, not merely completing exercises. When the learner is ready, identify accessible, legitimate free resources and classify them by:
- availability of Roman/English transliteration or transcript;
- availability of Nepali audio;
- story length and vocabulary/grammar difficulty;
- children's/simple-reader level through progressively more mature narrative material.

Do not claim a resource has a Roman transcript or audio until that specific resource has been checked. Reading/listening to a story may be used as broad passive comprehension; unfamiliar words do not automatically become active-study items.

## Seven-step authentic story learning workflow

Apply these steps to a selected story, adapting or omitting a modality only when the resource genuinely lacks it. Do not show answers/transcripts prematurely.

1. **Select and prepare:** Choose a story appropriate to the learner's demonstrated level. Record resource, story title, source level/difficulty, URL, paragraph count if known, and whether Nepali text, English translation, Roman transcription and Nepali audio are actually available.
2. **Roman Nepali reading:** Present the story or a manageable section in Roman Nepali only. Do not display the English translation before the learner's attempt.
3. **Learner explains meaning:** Learner gives an English account of the complete section in their own words. Do not routinely add redundant questions after a complete explanation.
4. **Assess reading comprehension:** Evaluate main idea, events, sequence, key details, cause/effect and inferred meaning as appropriate. Identify what was missed or misunderstood; ask targeted questions only when useful.
5. **Listening attempt:** Where Nepali audio exists, have the learner listen without seeing the transcript first. Learner explains what they understood in English. Never count reading as listening. If no audio exists, record listening as unavailable for that story and use another resource/story for the listening component.
6. **Reveal transcript and explain gaps:** Only after the listening attempt, provide the verified Roman Nepali transcript aligned to the audio and explain relevant words/expressions the learner could not recognise. If the source text is Devanagari, derive the Roman version from the actual source, not from guesswork.
7. **Compare and record:** Compare reading and listening comprehension, identify whether gaps came from vocabulary, grammar, connected speech, speed or attention (only where evidence supports the diagnosis), record useful new passive vocabulary in context if requested, and update the exercise log and next-story decision.

**Important:** English translation is a support/check after the learner's attempt, not a substitute for Nepali comprehension. The aim is to understand Nepali directly, with Roman script as the chosen reading support.
